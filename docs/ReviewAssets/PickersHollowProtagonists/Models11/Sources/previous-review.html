A

Storybook Warmth

Soft brown contours, rounded faces and broad quiet shading.

The most coherent and welcoming pair in this batch. Their faces read more clearly at native size. The body/clothing shapes still need a little more individuality.

Strongest warm face

Works: Rounded cheek planes, restrained eyes and a readable small smile. The broader adult body gives the workwear room to read.

Wrong / weak: The crown is still rather dome-shaped; the gloves and cuffs are oversized.

Refine: Break the crown into fewer directional locks and reduce the heavy glove shapes.

Most successful female face

Works: A readable smile and freckled cheeks; auburn ponytail distinguishes her silhouette without relying on outfit color.

Wrong / weak: Bang/cheek shadows are busy and the ponytail could be simpler. Her torso is nearly as broad as his. The red hair tie is not clearly separated from the auburn hair at native size.

Refine: Simplify the face-framing hair and separate the slightly smaller body silhouette.

B

Rustbelt Charm

More angular workwear, heavier selective outlines and sharper hair tufts.

Best match for the salvage-town occupation, particularly the male. The female face misses the brief; this is a promising silhouette direction, not a successful complete pair.

Strong salvage identity

Works: Distinct crown tufts, sturdy stance and a boxy overshirt communicate a scrappy warehouse repairman.

Wrong / weak: The tufts approach spikes; black gloves and outlines pull attention away from the face.

Refine: Soften the top tufts and reduce the glove/outline contrast without losing the stronger silhouette.

Face unsuccessful

Works: The cropped jacket, rolled cuffs and work boots make a strong practical silhouette.

Wrong / weak: Eye whites merge into a visor-like strip, and the hair reads like a cap. One isolated pixel remains. The head edit looked sleepier and introduced more stray pixels, so it was rejected.

Refine: A new face/hair solution is needed before this can become a master reference. Do not approve it on the strength of the outfit.

C

Expressive Indie

Lighter outlines, more directional curves and stronger facial contrast.

Some playful asymmetry came through, but the requested graphic identity is underdeveloped. It still leans toward a generic RPG look; I would not spend more generations on it before your choice.

Too wide-eyed / youthful

Works: Lighter outlines and directional hair make the face more prominent than B. The narrow shape rhythm differs from A.

Wrong / weak: Bright eye whites and orange cheek contrast make him look younger and more generic than intended. The hooked forelock is not distinctive enough.

Refine: Reduce eye-white and cheek contrast, retain an adult jaw, and commit to a clearer graphic hair shape.

Partial head refinement

Works: The head edit reduces the ponytail mass and removes the isolated pixel. The curved fringe and compact side hook give her an asymmetrical silhouette.

Wrong / weak: The fringe still crowds one eye; face highlights remain too bright. The red tie is subtle at native size, and the pair's boot baselines differ.

Refine: Open the eye area, simplify the hair planes and settle shared source alignment if this direction is selected.

Art findings, provenance and export checks

The older attempts often combined small heads with dense eye whites, brow shadows and tiny realistic facial marks. That produced hard bars, tired eyes or blank dot faces at native size. Hair was repeatedly treated as texture or a replacement patch rather than a designed silhouette. Copying one NPC face also limited the protagonists’ individual identities.

These six begin from new full-body generation. A uses rounded shapes and quiet shading, B stronger outlines and workwear planes, C lighter outlines and directional curves. Actual common-NPC artwork anchored pixel readability; each new male then anchored his matched female. Rejected protagonists were not generation references. The town art informed palette context and is only a display backdrop.

All six fit exact 48×64 transparent frames without local stretching, downscaling, clipping or hand-drawn corrections. Source PNGs are preserved. Both edits were generated through PixelLab masks; every unmasked RGBA pixel is unchanged. The rejected B edit remains separate.

Known limitations: B female retains an isolated pixel and a weak face. C’s pair has different boot baselines, and the female’s head edit uses more colors than the male. A fills more of the canvas than B, leaving only two-pixel vertical margins on the male; future animation framing needs care. Head/body ratios and palette temperature are art studies, not locked production contracts. Native facings, shared origins, mirrored accessories and animation consistency remain unproven. PixelLab lint also reports small color clusters and outline/banding information; export checks do not grant art approval.
